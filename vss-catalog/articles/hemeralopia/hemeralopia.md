## What is hemeralopia?

Hemeralopia, often called day blindness, describes vision that becomes markedly worse in bright conditions. The term is used inconsistently: in clinical contexts it commonly refers to severe glare sensitivity and reduced central vision associated with cone or cone–rod dysfunction. It is a symptom, not a single diagnosis, and can occur in different retinal disorders.

Typical difficulties can include photophobia, reduced visual acuity, impaired color discrimination, and slow recovery after glare. The exact combination depends on the underlying condition.

## Simulation

The preset approximates a severe bright-light impairment through the simulator's achromatopsia model, reducing color information and adding blur. This is an illustrative approximation. It cannot represent adaptation, eye movements, the cause of the symptom, or an affected person's complete visual experience.

## Assessment

Pronounced light sensitivity or vision loss in bright surroundings warrants an ophthalmological assessment. Examination can include visual acuity and color testing, retinal imaging, visual fields, and electroretinography depending on the suspected cause.
