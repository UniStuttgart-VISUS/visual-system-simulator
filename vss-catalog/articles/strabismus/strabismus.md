## What is strabismus?

Strabismus is a misalignment of the eyes: both eyes do not point at the same target at the same time. In esotropia, one or both eyes turn inward toward the nose. The deviation can be constant or intermittent and may affect one eye or alternate between the eyes.

Because the two eyes provide differently aligned images, strabismus can impair binocular cooperation and stereoscopic vision. A newly acquired esotropia in older children or adults can cause double vision. During childhood, the brain may instead suppress the image from one eye, which can contribute to amblyopia.

## Simulation

The preset offsets the virtual viewing axes in opposite directions to approximate a mild inward deviation. It demonstrates geometric image disparity only. It does not reproduce neural suppression, adaptation, amblyopia, or the full variability of an individual visual experience.

## Assessment and treatment

Persistent or newly appearing eye misalignment should be assessed by an eye-care professional. Treatment depends on the cause and can include glasses, amblyopia treatment, prisms, eye-muscle surgery, or a combination of approaches.
